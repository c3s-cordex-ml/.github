# C3S–CORDEX-ML

## About the organization

**C3S–CORDEX-ML** is a community developing open and reproducible machine-learning tools for regional climate downscaling, with a particular focus on regional climate model emulators (see [Kendon et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1175/BAMS-D-24-0114.1)). The community is supported by the [Copernicus Climate Change Service (C3S)](https://climate.copernicus.eu/) contract *Downscaling of CMIP6 Climate Projections Using Machine Learning Tools to Fill the Gaps of the Worldwide CORDEX RCM Simulations*, which runs until May 2028.

The project is closely connected to the [CORDEX Machine Learning initiative](https://cordex.org/strategic-activities/taskforces/task-force-on-machine-learning/), which coordinates machine-learning-based downscaling activities across the international CORDEX community. By bringing together climate scientists, machine-learning researchers, software developers, and data providers, we aim to:

- develop and evaluate machine-learning emulators of regional climate models, with a particular focus on continental-wide CORDEX domains;
- complement the CORDEX-CMIP6 ensemble by filling gaps in the domain–scenario–GCM–RCM matrix with RCM emulators;
- promote common protocols, transparent evaluation, and reproducible workflows; and
- make methods, code, and documentation reusable by the wider climate community.

The repositories in this organization are developed openly and collaboratively in alignment with CORDEX activities. Our goal is to ensure that these resources remain useful, maintainable, and accessible to the community beyond the lifetime of the C3S contract.

## Repositories

- **ml.evaluation** — Tools for evaluating machine-learning-based climate downscaling models. This repository builds on and expands the evaluation metrics and diagnostics recommended by CORDEX-ML-Bench for assessing machine-learning-based regional climate downscaling models.

## Contribution guidelines

Community contributions are welcome. You can get involved by:

- reporting bugs, unclear documentation, or reproducibility problems through the relevant repository's issue tracker;
- submitting fixes or new functionality through pull requests; and
- sharing results and lessons learned from applying these tools to other CORDEX domains.

Before contributing, please:

1. Read the repository-specific `README` and license files.
2. Search existing issues and pull requests to avoid duplicating work.
3. Open an issue before making a substantial change so that its scope and design can be discussed.

For questions that do not fit an existing issue, start a discussion in the most relevant repository or contact its maintainers.
